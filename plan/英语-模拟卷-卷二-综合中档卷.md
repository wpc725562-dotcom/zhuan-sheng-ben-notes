It is important to note that the Japanese also avoid direct eye contact during a bow, as it can be seen as aggressive.

In Thailand, people greet each other with a gesture called the "wai." This involves pressing your palms together at chest level and bowing your head slightly. The wai is not only a greeting but also a way to show respect. The higher your hands are placed, the more respect you are showing. However, it is considered impolite to return a wai to a child or a person of lower social status.

In France, a light kiss on both cheeks, known as "la bise," is a common greeting among friends and family. The number of kisses varies by region — in Paris, it is usually two, while in some southern regions, it can be three or four. It is important to know that la bise is only used between people who know each other well; a handshake is more appropriate for formal or first-time meetings.

Understanding these cultural differences can help avoid misunderstandings when traveling or doing business internationally. As the world becomes more connected, knowing how to greet people properly is a valuable skill.
